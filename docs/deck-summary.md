# Pathwise — Pitch Deck Summary (17 slides)

A slide-by-slide summary of the current Pathwise pitch deck. See
`docs/concept.md` for the fully expanded product concept these slides are
drawn from.

## Slide 1 — Title

**PATHWISE.** *Know the route. Not just the neighborhood.* Location
intelligence for safer, more informed journeys.

## Slide 2 — The Problem

Navigation tells you *how* to get there. Crime maps tell you *what happened*
nearby. Neither answers the question that actually matters: *what should I
know about my actual journey?*

## Slide 3 — The Solution

**Pathwise Route Intelligence.** Address → Destination → Time → Transportation
mode. The output explains the factors behind a route, rather than reducing it
to a single mysterious score.

## Slide 4 — Core Product

Walking route analysis, public-transit analysis, time-of-day context,
alternative route suggestions, explainable route considerations, and
shareable reports.

## Slide 5 — The Killer Feature

**"Why this route?"** — explains pedestrian infrastructure, major crossings,
activity/commercial context, historical incident context, transit
characteristics, and time-of-day factors behind every report.

## Slide 6 — Four Products

- **Pathwise Go** — everyday route intelligence
- **Pathwise Stay** — hotel and travel intelligence
- **Pathwise Home** — homebuyer and neighborhood intelligence
- **Pathwise Commute** — home-to-office mobility intelligence

## Slide 7 — Pathwise Home

Turns an address into a "living here" report: school routes, grocery access,
parks, restaurants, transit, likely commute, walking conditions, and
time-of-day considerations.

## Slide 8 — Personalization

Users choose what matters to them: fewer major crossings, stronger
pedestrian infrastructure, better lighting, higher activity/population,
lower or higher weight on historical incidents, shorter routes, or fewer
transfers. **Family mode** adapts routes for stroller-friendly, child-oriented
travel.

## Slide 9 — Data & Technology

**Pathwise Intelligence Engine** = maps + routing APIs + public data +
transit data + pedestrian infrastructure + time/context. The proprietary
asset is the intelligence layer that fuses these sources — not the base map.

## Slide 10 — Competitive Positioning

Google Maps = navigation. Crime maps = incident visualization. Walkability
tools = area-level context. Safety apps = often walking-only. Real estate
platforms = property info. **Pathwise = journey-level, multi-modal context
across travel, real estate, and commuting.**

## Slide 11 — Business Model

**Consumer:** free basic analysis, Pathwise Plus subscription (~$7.99/mo),
one-time Home/Moving reports. **B2B:** realtor subscriptions, hotel and
hospitality tools, corporate relocation packages, API/white-label location
intelligence.

## Slide 12 — Go-To-Market

Start with one metro — potential pilot: **Dallas–Fort Worth**. Initial
audience: homebuyers, families, travelers, commuters, and women traveling
alone. Expand once repeat usage and willingness to pay are proven.

## Slide 13 — MVP

Address search, destination search, walking + transit routes, time
selection, route considerations, alternative route, explanation of factors,
save/share report. **Deferred:** live companion mode, social features,
nationwide coverage.

## Slide 14 — Ethical & Trust Principles

Focus strictly on places, routes, and observable public data. Never
characterize individuals, homes, races, ethnic groups, or protected classes
as dangerous. Avoid false precision — explain evidence and uncertainty
rather than declaring a route flatly "safe" or "unsafe."

## Slide 15 — The Moat

A proprietary location-intelligence dataset built over time: user
preferences, route comparisons, infrastructure context, time-dependent
patterns, and data-quality signals, compounding into city-specific route
intelligence. Long-term opportunity: infrastructure other companies can
license and build on.

## Slide 16 — Vision

**Pathwise = Location Intelligence.** *"What should I know about this place
and the journey to or from it?"* — applicable across Travel, Real Estate,
Hotels, Mobility, Relocation, Families, and Universities.

## Slide 17 — Next Steps

Validate name/domain/trademarks, design 10–12 core screens, select mapping
and data providers, build a Dallas–Fort Worth prototype, test with
homebuyers and travelers, measure repeat use and paid conversion, and develop
the B2B/API offering. (Full detail in `docs/roadmap.md`.)
